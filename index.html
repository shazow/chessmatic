How the battle plays out

Battles run in rounds with a fixed batting order — and the house plays White, so it moves first: their first piece, your first, their second, your second, and so on. The schedule never changes, so you can plan around it. Every piece attempts one action per round; a frozen piece simply does nothing in its slot. If a whole round passes with no piece moving, the position is dead and the house wins. Every piece follows the same three rules, always:

1. Strike. If it can capture, it captures the most valuable target in range. Full chess range — a rook strikes down its whole file or rank.

2. Creep. Otherwise it advances one square toward the nearest enemy. Knights leap as normal.

3. Never trade down. A piece refuses to move onto — or capture on — any square defended by a cheaper or equal enemy. A 2-point pawn's diagonal is poison to a 5-point rook. A piece with no safe option must still act — it makes its least-bad move, which is how baiting works. (Enable holds in Advanced settings and trapped pieces will freeze in place instead.)

Win by clearing every enemy piece within 20 rounds. Your score is the points you spent — match par and you've found the cheapest possible answer.

Advanced settings

By default every piece must act: anything that would stand still makes its least-bad move instead. Turning this on is the club rules variant — pieces may hold their ground, and a piece with no safe move is pinned (⊘) until the position changes. Every par changes with the rule; the par display, spoilers, and share text all follow whichever is active.
