How the battle plays out

Set your lineup, then watch it play. Initiative is fixed before battle. Each side is numbered from its front line (closest to the enemy) to its back line; pieces on the same file use the lower rank first. The badges show this order during setup. The house plays White and takes the first slot, then the sides alternate: house #1, yours #1, house #2, yours #2, and so on. The schedule locks when battle starts, so moves and captures never reorder it — the same setup always plays the same way.

Each surviving piece gets one action slot per round; a frozen piece skips its slot. If no piece moves for a full round, the position is dead and the house wins. On its slot, every piece follows these rules in order:

1. Strike. If it has an allowed capture, it takes the most valuable target in range. Pieces use their full chess range — for example, a rook can strike anywhere along an open file or rank.

2. Creep. Otherwise, it looks for a safe move that gets it closer to the nearest enemy. Sliding pieces creep one square; knights use their usual leap.

3. Never trade down. A destination is unsafe if it is defended by an equal-or-cheaper enemy, or if the moving piece would be attacked and left undefended. A capture can override that caution when the target is worth at least as much as the attacker.

Under standard rules, a piece that cannot creep safely makes its least-bad legal move — even onto a dangerous square. That forced movement makes baiting possible. Under club rules, pieces may hold instead, and a piece with no safe move is pinned (⊘) until the position changes.

Clear every enemy piece within 20 rounds to win. Your score is the total points you spent. Match the displayed par and you've found the cheapest possible answer for the active rules.

Advanced settings

By default every piece must act: anything that would stand still makes its least-bad move instead. Turning this on is the club rules variant — pieces may hold their ground, and a piece with no safe move is pinned (⊘) until the position changes. Every par changes with the rule; the par display, spoilers, and share text all follow whichever is active.
